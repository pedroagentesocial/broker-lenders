Somewhere between pre-approval and closing, some buyers hear a request they didn't expect: "we'll need your homebuyer education certificate." It isn't a test and nobody fails it. But on certain loans it's a real condition, and if nobody mentions it until the last week, a few hours of class can end up delaying your closing.

## A course and counseling aren't the same thing

<div class="bl-versus">
  <div class="bl-versus-card is-a">
    <p class="bl-versus-head">Homebuyer education</p>
    <p class="bl-versus-big">A course</p>
    <ul class="bl-versus-list">
      <li>Online, by phone, in person or a mix</li>
      <li>Covers budgeting, credit, the loan process and owning a home</li>
      <li>Ends with a certificate</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-versus-card is-b">
    <p class="bl-versus-head">Housing counseling</p>
    <p class="bl-versus-big">One on one</p>
    <ul class="bl-versus-list">
      <li>With a counselor at a HUD-approved agency</li>
      <li>Looks at your own budget, credit and goals</li>
      <li>Often at little or no cost</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

A course teaches the process in general. Counseling looks at your situation. For Fannie Mae loans, counseling from a HUD-approved agency can be used instead of the course when the loan requires education.

## When your loan requires it

Most buyers don't need either one to get a mortgage. Fannie Mae requires that at least one borrower complete homeownership education before closing in these cases:

<div class="bl-table-wrap">
  <table class="bl-table">
    <thead><tr><th>Situation</th><th>Education required?</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>HomeReady purchase where every borrower who will live in the home is a first-time homebuyer</td><td class="bl-yes">Yes</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Purchase with more than 95% financing (LTV, CLTV or HCLTV) where every borrower is a first-time homebuyer</td><td class="bl-yes">Yes</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Purchase run through Fannie Mae's automated system where no borrower has a single credit account</td><td class="bl-yes">Yes</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Manually underwritten purchase where no borrower has a credit score</td><td class="bl-yes">Yes</td></tr>
      <tr><td>The same loans when at least one borrower has owned a home recently</td><td class="bl-no">Not for that reason</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

Being a "first-time homebuyer" here is broader than it sounds: generally, you count as one if you haven't owned a home in the past three years, even if you owned one before that. So someone who sold a house five years ago and has rented since can fall under the rule.

Outside Fannie Mae, many down payment assistance programs ask for a homebuyer course too, each with its own rules about which providers they accept. If you're using assistance, ask the program which course it takes before you sign up for one. Reverse mortgages have their own required counseling, which we cover in [our reverse mortgage guide](/blog/reverse-mortgages-explained/).

## What makes a course count

For Fannie Mae, the course can come from any qualified provider that's independent of the lender, as long as its content is aligned with the National Industry Standards for homeownership education or HUD's standards. It can be online, in person, by phone or a combination. Fannie Mae's own free online course, HomeView, is one option. The lender has to keep your certificate of completion in the loan file, so save it as a PDF the day you finish.

There's one more reason to look at counseling on a HomeReady loan: borrowers who complete housing counseling within 12 months before closing may be eligible for a pricing credit on the loan. Ask your loan officer whether it applies to yours before you choose between a course and counseling.

## The list your lender has to give you

Even when your loan doesn't require any of this, federal rules make sure you know where to go. Within **three business days** after a lender or mortgage broker gets your application, it has to give you a list of homeownership counseling organizations near you, built from data published by the CFPB or HUD. The only exceptions are reverse mortgages, loans secured by a timeshare, and applications that are denied or withdrawn before the three days are up.

That list is easy to ignore in the stack of early disclosures. It's worth keeping, because the same agencies help long after closing.

## How to find a counselor yourself

The CFPB's **Find a Housing Counselor** tool searches HUD-approved agencies by ZIP code, and you can also call the CFPB at (855) 411-2372. According to the CFPB, these counselors can give you independent advice, often at little or no cost. Depending on the agency, they help with buying a home, renting, credit problems, forbearance, default and avoiding foreclosure. Not every agency offers every service, so check each one's list.
