Student loans don't stop you from buying a home. What they do is change your debt-to-income ratio — and here's the part almost nobody is told: **the monthly payment the lender uses for your student loans is set by the loan program's rules, not by your bill.**

If you're paying the full amount every month, it's simple: the lender uses that payment. It gets interesting when your loans are deferred, in forbearance, or on an income-driven plan with a very low or $0 payment. That's where programs disagree, and where the right choice can be worth tens of thousands of dollars of house.

## Same loans, four different answers

Take $60,000 of student loans that are currently deferred, so your credit report shows no payment. This is the payment each program makes the lender count:

## What that's worth in house

Every $100 a month of debt the lender counts is roughly $100 a month less mortgage payment you can qualify for. At a 6.5% rate, that's about **$15,800 of loan** per $100.

## The rules, program by program

      <tr><td>Paying a full, regular payment</td><td>That payment</td><td>That payment</td><td>That payment</td><td>That payment*</td></tr>

      <tr><td>Income-driven plan, documented $0</td><td class="bl-yes">$0</td><td>0.5% of balance</td><td>0.5% of balance</td><td>5% ÷ 12*</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Deferred or in forbearance</td><td>1% of balance, or the amortizing payment</td><td>0.5% of balance</td><td>0.5% of balance</td><td class="bl-yes">Excluded if deferred 12+ months past closing; otherwise 5% ÷ 12</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>

*On VA loans, if the payment on your credit report is lower than 5% of the balance divided by 12, the lender uses that threshold — unless you document a different actual payment with a statement from your servicer.

## Four things to check before you apply

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Log in to StudentAid.gov</b> Confirm every federal loan, its balance and its status. Private loans won't be there — check those with each lender.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Know your plan and your payment</b> Repayment plans have changed several times in recent years. What matters to underwriting is what your servicer shows on a current statement.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Ask which investor your loan will go to</b> Two conventional loans can count your student loans differently depending on whether they follow Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac rules. Your loan officer can run both.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Don't let loans go delinquent</b> A late or defaulted federal student loan is a much bigger problem than the balance. Defaulted federal debt can make you ineligible for FHA and VA financing until it's resolved.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Don't refinance student loans right before buying</b> Moving federal loans into a private loan gives up income-driven options and can raise the payment the lender counts. Talk to me first.</span></div>
</div>
