You have equity, and you need cash — for a kitchen, an addition, a business, or to wipe out high-interest debt. There are two main ways to get it, and they're opposites:

- A **cash-out refinance** replaces your whole mortgage with a new, larger one at today's rate. You get the difference in cash.
- A **HELOC** — home equity line of credit — leaves your mortgage untouched and adds a second loan on top, which you draw from as you need it.

The question that decides between them is almost always the same: **what's the rate on the mortgage you already have?**

## The same $60,000, two ways

A home worth $500,000. You owe $250,000 at 3.5%, with a principal and interest payment of $1,347. You need $60,000. The rates below are examples — HELOCs are usually variable and priced above first mortgages.

Here's the cost hiding inside the cash-out option: you'd be re-pricing $250,000 of debt from 3.5% to 6.5% just to borrow $60,000. That's roughly **$7,500 a year in extra interest** on money you'd already borrowed — before you even count the new $60,000.

## How much you can take out

      <tr><td>HELOC</td><td>Commonly 80% to 90% of value, combined with your first mortgage — set by each lender</td><td>Both loans together up to $400,000–$450,000</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

Seasoning matters too. For a conventional cash-out, Fannie Mae requires the mortgage being paid off to be at least 12 months old, and at least one borrower to have been on title for six months.

## When each one wins

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>HELOC wins when your current rate is low</b> Protecting a 3% or 4% first mortgage is usually worth more than anything a refinance offers.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>HELOC wins when you need money in stages</b> A remodel paid over six months, or an emergency reserve you might not use. You only pay interest on what you draw.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Cash-out wins when today's rate is at or below yours</b> Then you lower your rate and get cash in the same transaction — one loan, one fixed payment.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Cash-out wins when you need a large, fixed amount and certainty</b> A variable HELOC rate is a risk you carry for years; a fixed refinance isn't.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Either way, it's your house on the line</b> Turning credit card debt into home debt lowers the rate, but it changes unsecured debt into debt secured by your home. Only do it with a plan to not run the cards back up.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="bl-callout">
  <p class="bl-callout-title">Pedro's note</p>
  <p>Two things people learn too late. First, on your primary home you get three business days to cancel after signing either one, so the money doesn't arrive the day you sign — plan for it. Second, the interest is only tax-deductible if you use the money to buy, build or substantially improve the home that secures the loan. Paying off a car or a credit card doesn't qualify. Ask your tax preparer before you count on the deduction.</p>
</div>
