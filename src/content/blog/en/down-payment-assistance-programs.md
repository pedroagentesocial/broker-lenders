The down payment is the biggest obstacle for most first-time buyers. What many of them don't know is that every state has a public housing agency whose job includes helping with exactly that. Cities and counties often have programs too.

The help is real. It also comes with rules, and the right program depends on your income, the home and your plans.

## The main types of help

<div class="bl-table-wrap">
  <table class="bl-table">
    <thead><tr><th>Type</th><th>How it works</th><th>What to watch</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>Grant</td><td class="bl-yes">Money you don't pay back</td><td>Often has income limits, and may have conditions if you sell or refinance soon</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Forgivable second loan</td><td>A second loan that's forgiven over time if you stay in the home</td><td>If you sell or refinance before it's forgiven, you may owe part of it</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Deferred second loan</td><td>No monthly payment; you repay it when you sell, refinance or pay off the first loan</td><td>It's still debt. Plan for it when you sell</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Repayable second loan</td><td>A small second loan with a monthly payment</td><td>Its payment counts in your debt-to-income ratio</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Mortgage Credit Certificate (MCC)</td><td>A federal tax credit for part of the mortgage interest you pay each year</td><td>Doesn't help with cash at closing; it lowers your taxes after</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>

## How it fits with your loan

Most state programs are paired with an FHA or conventional first mortgage from a lender approved by the agency. FHA allows secondary financing from government agencies and certain public entities, which is how many of these programs work. On conventional loans, there are specific rules for this kind of "community second" financing as well.

## Mortgage Credit Certificates

An MCC is issued by a state or local agency, and it gives you a federal tax credit for a percentage of the mortgage interest you pay each year. If the credit rate is above 20%, the credit is capped at $2,000 a year. If you itemize, you have to reduce your mortgage interest deduction by the amount of the credit.

One catch: if you sell a home financed with an MCC or a bond-financed mortgage, you may owe a **recapture tax** in some cases (IRS Form 8828). Ask the agency and a tax professional how it would apply to you.

## The questions to ask before you sign

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Do I qualify?</b> Income limits, purchase price limits, first-time buyer requirements and minimum credit scores vary by program.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Do I have to pay it back, and when?</b> When you sell, when you refinance, after a number of years, or never.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>What does it do to my rate and costs?</b> Some programs come with a higher rate on the first loan. Compare the full package with and without help.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Is there a homebuyer course?</b> Many programs require one, so take it early.</span></div>
