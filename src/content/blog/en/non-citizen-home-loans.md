"Can I buy a house if I'm not a citizen?" Yes. Plenty of people do it every year. But the answer to "which loan?" depends on your status, and one of the rules changed recently: FHA, the program many first-time buyers use, closed its doors to non-permanent residents in 2025.

This isn't immigration or legal advice. It's a map of what each loan program requires, so you can ask the right questions.

## The FHA change: what happened in 2025

In March 2025, HUD issued Mortgagee Letter 2025-09. It removed the "non-permanent resident" category from FHA completely. Lenders had to apply it to every FHA case number assigned on or after **May 25, 2025**. It covers FHA purchase and refinance loans and reverse mortgages (HECM). The letter also updated the rules for FHA streamline refinances.

Since then, FHA accepts three groups:

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>U.S. citizens.</b></span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Lawful permanent residents,</b> on the same terms as citizens. The file must include evidence of permanent residence, and the application must say you're a permanent resident.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Citizens of the Federated States of Micronesia, the Republic of the Marshall Islands or the Republic of Palau,</b> with evidence of that citizenship.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>A Social Security card isn't enough.</b> HUD says it doesn't prove immigration or work status. Separately, each borrower must give the lender evidence of a valid Social Security number.</span></div>
</div>

The effect was immediate. In an October 2026 release, HUD said non-permanent residents went from about 5.8% of FHA purchase loans before the change to about 0.1% after.

## Conventional loans: the door that's still open

Fannie Mae's rule is short. It buys mortgages made to non-citizens who are **lawful permanent or non-permanent residents**, on the same terms available to U.S. citizens. Fannie Mae doesn't list specific documents. The lender decides, case by case, what it needs to confirm your status, and when it sells the loan it promises Fannie Mae that you're legally present in the country.

That means two lenders can ask for different papers. Have your passport, visa or employment authorization document ready from the start. If you have a work visa and were planning on FHA, a conventional loan is the place to look now. [You don't need 20% down](/blog/down-payment-myths/) shows how low the down payment can go.

## USDA and other programs

USDA's guaranteed loan program has its own rule in federal regulation: applicants must provide evidence that they are **U.S. citizens, U.S. non-citizen nationals or qualified aliens**, as the regulation defines them. "Qualified alien" is a legal category, so ask the lender to check your specific documents against it. [USDA loans, explained](/blog/usda-loans-explained/) covers the rest of the program.

And if you only have an ITIN? Some lenders offer ITIN loans with their own rules, outside FHA and Fannie Mae. Rates, down payments and fees vary a lot, so compare the Loan Estimate line by line before you commit.
