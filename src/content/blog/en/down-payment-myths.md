I have had this conversation hundreds of times. Someone tells me they're "still saving" and they've been saving for four years. I ask how much they need. They say twenty percent. Nobody ever told them where that number came from.

It came from nowhere. It is not a rule, it is not a law, and it is not what most of my clients put down.

A few notes that matter more than the table suggests:

- **Conventional 3% is not for everyone.** The 97% loan-to-value options generally require at least one first-time buyer, or they run through HomeReady, which caps qualifying income at 80% of the area median. If neither fits, 5% is your conventional floor.
- **FHA's 580 line is real.** At 580 you're at 3.5% down. At 579 you're at 10%. One point of credit score is worth $26,000 on a $400,000 house.
- **VA has no down payment and no monthly mortgage insurance.** If you served and you're not using it, you are leaving the best loan in the country on the table.

## So what does 20% actually buy you?

Three real things: no PMI, a smaller loan, and an offer that looks strong to a seller. Those are worth something. They're just rarely worth the wait.

## Where the money can come from

The down payment does not have to be money you personally saved. What matters is that it's documented and that the source is allowed.

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Gift funds</b> From family on most programs, with a signed gift letter and a paper trail showing where it came from.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Down payment assistance</b> Every state I'm licensed in runs programs — grants, second mortgages, forgivable loans.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Retirement accounts</b> Loans and hardship withdrawals from a 401(k) or IRA, subject to your plan's rules and the tax consequences.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Sale of an asset</b> A car, a second property, stock — as long as you can show the sale and the deposit.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Not: unsourced cash</b> Money that appears in your account without a trace gets excluded from your assets. Deposit it 60+ days before you apply.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Not: seller credits</b> A seller can pay your closing costs, within program limits — but a seller cannot fund your down payment.</span></div>
</div>
