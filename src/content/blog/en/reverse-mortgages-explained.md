A reverse mortgage is a loan against your home for homeowners 62 and older. Instead of you paying the lender every month, the lender pays you, as a lump sum, monthly payments or a line of credit. You keep the title, and you don't make a monthly mortgage payment.

It can be a useful tool. But it isn't free money: interest and fees are added to the balance every month, so the amount you owe **goes up over time**, and your equity goes down.

The most common type of reverse mortgage is the **HECM** (Home Equity Conversion Mortgage), insured by FHA. That's what this guide covers.

## Who qualifies

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>You're 62 or older.</b></span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>You own the home outright or have a small mortgage balance.</b></span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>It's your principal residence,</b> where you live most of the year.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>You're not behind on any federal debt.</b></span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>You can keep paying property taxes, homeowners insurance and HOA dues.</b></span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>You complete counseling</b> with an FHA-approved counselor. Agencies can charge a reasonable fee, but not if you can't afford it.</span></div>
</div>

## How much you can get

The amount you can borrow is called the **principal limit**. It depends on the age of the youngest borrower, the interest rate and the home's value, capped at the FHA limit. Older borrowers, higher-value homes and lower rates mean a higher limit.

For FHA case numbers assigned in 2026, the limit is **$1,249,125** nationwide, up from $1,209,750 in 2025. That's not the most you can borrow. It's the highest home value the calculation can use. A $1.5 million home is figured as if it were worth $1,249,125.

You can also combine a line of credit with monthly payments.

## What it costs

HECMs have closing costs like any mortgage, plus FHA mortgage insurance. The insurance is what protects you and your heirs if the loan ends up bigger than the house is worth.

## Your obligations don't go away

No monthly mortgage payment doesn't mean no bills. You still have to pay the **property taxes, homeowners insurance** (and flood insurance or HOA dues, if any), and keep the home in good repair. Falling behind on any of these is a default, and a default can lead to foreclosure. This is the most common way reverse mortgage borrowers get in trouble.

## When the loan comes due

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>When the last borrower dies, sells the home or moves out</b> as their main residence.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>After more than 12 consecutive months in a care facility,</b> if no co-borrower still lives in the home. Anyone else living there would have to move out unless they can pay off the loan or qualify as an eligible non-borrowing spouse.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>A spouse who isn't on the loan</b> may be able to stay after the borrower dies, as an eligible non-borrowing spouse, if they meet HUD's rules. It depends on when the loan was taken out, and it can be hard to qualify. Getting both spouses on the loan avoids the problem, if both are 62.</span></div>
</div>

When the loan comes due, the home is usually sold to pay it off. Heirs who want to keep the house have to pay the full balance. If the home is worth less than the balance, they can sell it and pay the balance or 95% of the appraised value, whichever is less, and FHA insurance covers the rest. Heirs have 30 days after the due-and-payable notice, and that can sometimes be extended up to six months. [Inheriting a home that still has a mortgage](/blog/inheriting-a-home-with-a-mortgage/) covers the rest of what families face.

## Red flags

Be wary of anyone who pushes a reverse mortgage to pay for home repairs they're selling. The VA does not offer reverse mortgages, so an ad saying otherwise is a warning sign. And for most reverse mortgages you have **three business days after closing** to cancel for any reason, in writing.
