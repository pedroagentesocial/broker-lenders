A job loss, an illness, a divorce. Any of them can make a mortgage payment impossible for a while. The worst thing you can do is go quiet. The best thing is to call your servicer early, before you're far behind, because that's when you have the most options.

Federal rules also give you real protections. Knowing them takes away a lot of the fear.

## Apply for help: the "loss mitigation application"

The formal way to ask for help is a **loss mitigation application**: the forms and documents your servicer asks for so it can review you for every available option. The rules protect you once you apply:

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Acknowledgment within 5 business days</b> The servicer must tell you in writing that it received your application and whether it's complete or what's missing.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>A decision within 30 days</b> If your complete application arrives more than 37 days before a foreclosure sale, the servicer must evaluate you for all available options and answer in writing within 30 days.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>No "dual tracking"</b> If your complete application arrives before foreclosure starts, the servicer generally can't file for foreclosure while it's being decided.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>The right to appeal</b> If your complete application arrives 90 or more days before a sale and a loan modification is denied, you have 14 days to appeal, and someone different must review it.</span></div>
</div>

The key word is **complete**. Send everything they ask for, keep copies, and write down every call: the date, the person and what was said.

## The options

<div class="bl-table-wrap">
  <table class="bl-table">
    <thead><tr><th>Option</th><th>What it does</th><th>Good for</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>Forbearance</td><td>Pauses or reduces payments for a set time. You still owe the full amount afterward.</td><td>A short, temporary problem</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Repayment plan</td><td>You catch up on what's behind by paying extra for a few months.</td><td>Your income is back and you can pay a bit more</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Loan modification</td><td>Permanently changes the loan's terms to make the payment affordable.</td><td>A longer-lasting drop in income</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Short sale or deed-in-lieu</td><td>You leave the home in an orderly way and avoid foreclosure.</td><td>When keeping the home isn't realistic</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

After forbearance, the missed amount has to be paid back. Depending on your servicer, that can be all at once, spread over higher payments for a while, or moved to the end of the loan. Ask exactly which option applies **before** you accept, because a lump sum you can't pay just postpones the problem.

<div class="bl-callout">
  <p class="bl-callout-title">Pedro's note</p>
  <p>Call a HUD-approved housing counselor. It's free, they know how servicers work, and they can help you fill out the application correctly. That's very different from anyone who calls you offering to "save your home" for a fee. Even if you end up selling, it's much better to sell before a foreclosure than after: your credit, and the wait to buy again, change a lot.</p>
</div>

## Watch out for foreclosure rescue scams

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Upfront fees to "save" your home</b> Don't pay anyone before they've done anything.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>"Stop paying your lender and pay us"</b> That's a scam, and it puts you further behind.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Asking you to sign over your deed</b> Never transfer your title to someone who promises to rent it back to you or "fix" the loan.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Guarantees</b> Nobody can guarantee a loan modification.</span></div>
</div>
