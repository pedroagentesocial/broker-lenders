A pre-qualification and a pre-approval both end with a PDF that says a number. That's where the similarity stops.

One is a conversation. The other is an underwriting file. In a market where a good listing gets three offers, the difference decides which one the seller picks.

## What each level actually verifies

The key line is the last one. A pre-approval says *you* are approved; what remains is the house. That's a very different sentence than "based on what you told me, you should be fine."

## What actually happens in a pre-approval

<div class="bl-steps">
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">1</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">Day 1 — 20 minutes</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">The application</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">Income, employment, assets, debts, and the property type you're looking for. This is the same form (the 1003) that will follow your file all the way to closing.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">2</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">Day 1</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">Credit pull — all three bureaus</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">A tri-merge report. The middle score is what prices your loan. Multiple mortgage inquiries made in a short shopping window are generally counted as one by the scoring models, so getting a second opinion is not the credit hit people fear.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">3</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">Days 1–2</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">Documents</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">Pay stubs, W-2s, tax returns if you're self-employed, two months of bank statements. This is where files stall — every day you take is a day your letter isn't ready.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step is-accent">
    <div class="bl-step-num">4</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">Days 2–3</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">Automated underwriting, then the letter</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">Your file runs through Desktop Underwriter or FHA's TOTAL Scorecard, which returns an actual decision with conditions. The letter that follows is backed by something.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="bl-callout">
  <p class="bl-callout-title">Pedro's note</p>
  <p>Ask for your pre-approval letter to show a number <em>below</em> your maximum. If you're approved to $520,000 and you're offering $465,000, a letter that says $520,000 just told the seller you can pay more. I write the letter at the offer amount. It costs nothing and it's saved my clients real money in negotiations.</p>
</div>

## What makes a letter strong

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Dated within 30 days</b> Agents read the date first. A letter from four months ago reads as "this buyer has been losing offers."</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Names the loan program and down payment</b> "Conventional, 10% down" tells a seller more than a dollar amount alone.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Has a real person's direct line on it</b> The agent will call it. If nobody answers, your offer moves down the pile.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Says credit, income and assets were verified</b> The specific wording matters. "Verified" is a different claim than "reviewed."</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Doesn't expire mid-escrow</b> Most letters run 60–90 days because credit and income documents age out. Ask when yours does.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Isn't your only conversation</b> A pre-approval is not a rate lock. The rate is set later, when you're under contract.</span></div>
</div>

## When to get one

Before you look at a single house. Not because the letter takes long — it doesn't — but because it's the only way to find the problems while there's still time to fix them. A collection you forgot about, a name mismatch, a deposit you can't document: every one of those is a two-week fix in November and a dead deal in March.
