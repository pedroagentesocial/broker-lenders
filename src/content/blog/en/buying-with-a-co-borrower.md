Your income is steady, your credit is fine, and the numbers still fall short. If a parent, sibling or other relative is willing to help, their income can sometimes close the gap.

It can be a good solution, and it's a serious commitment for them. Before anyone signs, both of you should know what the signature really means.

## How much difference it can make

Say you earn $4,500 a month and pay $300 in other debts. The home you want has a $2,300 monthly housing payment. Your parent earns $7,000 a month and has $2,100 in monthly obligations, including their own mortgage.

## Co-borrower or co-signer: what's the difference

The words get mixed up, but they're not the same thing. The key question is whether the person's name goes on the home's title.

<div class="bl-versus">
  <div class="bl-versus-card is-a">
    <p class="bl-versus-head">Non-occupant co-borrower</p>
    <p class="bl-versus-big">Owes the debt and can own the home</p>
    <ul class="bl-versus-list">
      <li>Signs the note and is fully responsible for the debt</li>
      <li>On FHA loans, must take title and sign the security instrument</li>
      <li>On Fannie Mae loans, may or may not be on the title</li>
      <li>Doesn't live in the home</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-versus-card is-b">
    <p class="bl-versus-head">Co-signer</p>
    <p class="bl-versus-big">Owes the debt, owns nothing</p>
    <ul class="bl-versus-list">
      <li>Signs the note and is fully responsible for the debt</li>
      <li>Has no ownership interest in the home</li>
      <li>Doesn't sign the security instrument on an FHA loan</li>
      <li>Takes all of the risk with none of the property rights</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

Neither role can be someone with an interest in the sale. The seller, the builder and the real estate agent can't fill it.

## The rules by program

<div class="bl-table-wrap">
  <table class="bl-table">
    <thead><tr><th>Rule</th><th>FHA</th><th>Conventional (Fannie Mae)</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>Maximum loan-to-value with a non-occupant</td><td class="bl-yes">96.5% if you're family; 75% if you aren't</td><td>95% with automated underwriting; 90% if manually underwritten</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Key limits</td><td>The 96.5% doesn't apply to two- to four-unit homes, or if a family member is selling to a family member who'll be the non-occupying co-borrower</td><td>Manual underwriting: the occupying borrower's own debt-to-income ratio can't exceed 43%</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Your own money</td><td>Follows the normal FHA down payment rules</td><td>Manual underwriting: the occupying borrower generally funds the first 5% unless the loan is 80% or less, or a gift or grant exception applies</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Residency</td><td>Non-occupying co-borrowers and co-signers must be U.S. citizens or have a principal residence in the U.S.</td><td>Reviewed under the loan's normal eligibility rules</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

That 43% limit matters. In our example, your own ratio is 57.8%, so a manually underwritten Fannie Mae loan wouldn't work. Automated underwriting looks at all borrowers together, so the same file might be approved. VA and USDA have their own rules on who can join a loan, so ask before you plan around them.

## What the helper is really taking on

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>They owe the entire debt</b> Not half. If you stop paying, the lender can come to them for all of it.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Late payments hit their credit too</b> The loan normally appears on their credit report, so a payment you miss can damage their score.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>It can block their next loan</b> The payment can count against their debt-to-income ratio. On FHA, it can usually be left out once they document that you've made 12 months of on-time payments.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>A co-signer gets no say in the home</b> Without being on the title, they carry the debt but have no ownership right to the property.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="bl-callout">
  <p class="bl-callout-title">Pedro's note</p>
  <p>Have the awkward conversation before the application. What happens if you lose your job? Who pays if there's a shortfall? If the home sells, who gets what? Write it down, even in a simple family agreement, and consider asking an attorney to review it. It's much easier to agree when everybody is happy. Also set up a way for the helper to see that payments are being made, so nobody has to ask.</p>
</div>

## How the helper gets off the loan

Signing is easy. Getting off is harder. A lender generally won't remove someone just because you ask, since they approved the loan based on that person's income.

<div class="bl-steps">
  <div class="bl-step is-accent">
    <div class="bl-step-num">1</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">The usual route</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">Refinance in your own name</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">Once your income has grown or your debts have dropped, you qualify alone for a new loan that pays off the old one. Today's rates and closing costs apply, so run the numbers first.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">2</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">If you're moving anyway</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">Sell the home</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">The sale pays off the loan and ends the obligation for everyone.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">3</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">Worth asking about</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">Ask the servicer about a release</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">Some servicers may review a release of liability. Don't count on it, and don't remove anyone from the title without checking how that affects the loan.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

Also remember that taking someone off the title doesn't take them off the debt. The note and the deed are separate.
