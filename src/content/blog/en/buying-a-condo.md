When you buy a house, the lender reviews you and that one property. When you buy a condo, the lender reviews you, your unit and **the entire project**: its budget, its savings, its insurance, its lawsuits and how many neighbors are behind on their dues.

That means you can be fully qualified and still get turned down because of the building. It's the most common surprise in condo buying, and you can usually find it out before you make an offer.

## The dues come straight out of your buying power

HOA dues count in your debt-to-income ratio as part of your housing payment. So the same monthly budget buys less.

## What the lender checks on the building

For a conventional loan, the usual path is a Full Review based on Fannie Mae's standards. These are the items that most often decide it:

<div class="bl-table-wrap">
  <table class="bl-table">
    <thead><tr><th>What's reviewed</th><th>The standard</th><th>Why it matters to you</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>Reserves</td><td>At least 10% of the budget goes to replacement reserves today, or a qualifying reserve study; 15% for loan applications dated on or after January 4, 2027</td><td>Low savings today means special assessments tomorrow</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Late dues</td><td>No more than 15% of units can be 60 or more days behind</td><td>If neighbors don't pay, the budget falls short</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Single owner</td><td>Limits on one person or company: 2 units in a 5–20 unit project, 20% in projects of 21 or more</td><td>Too much control in a single owner's hands</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Commercial space</td><td>No more than 35% of the project or building</td><td>The property has to be mainly residential</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Lawsuits</td><td>No litigation over safety, structural soundness, habitability or functional use</td><td>The outcome could fall on the owners</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Critical repairs</td><td>No material deficiencies, including unfunded repairs over $10,000 per unit needed within 12 months</td><td>The project is ineligible until they're fixed</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

Some projects are ineligible by their nature, such as condo-hotels with hotel-style services or mandatory rental pools, timeshares, and projects with an evacuation order for unsafe conditions. A project that can't meet these standards is often called "non-warrantable." Financing may still exist with some lenders, but usually with a larger down payment and different terms.

## What changed in 2026, and what's coming

<div class="bl-steps">
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">1</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">August 3, 2026 · Already in effect</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">The shortcut review ended</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">Fannie Mae retired Limited Review, the quicker check many established condos relied on. Most projects now need a Full Review, so your lender will ask the HOA for more information.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">2</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">Already in effect</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">Small projects got easier</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">The waiver of project review now reaches condo projects of up to ten units. For five to ten units, the project can't be part of a larger development or master association.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step is-accent">
    <div class="bl-step-num">3</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">January 4, 2027 · Coming up</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">Reserves go from 10% to 15%</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">For loan applications under Full Review dated on or after this date, the project's budget must set aside at least 15%. A building that passes today might not pass in January if its board doesn't update its budget.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

## FHA and VA keep their own lists

Conventional approval doesn't carry over. Each government program has its own process.

<div class="bl-versus">
  <div class="bl-versus-card is-a">
    <p class="bl-versus-head">FHA</p>
    <p class="bl-versus-big">Project or single unit</p>
    <ul class="bl-versus-list">
      <li>Search the project on HUD's approved-condo list</li>
      <li>If it isn't approved, a single-unit approval may be possible</li>
      <li>That route needs a project of at least five units, and it limits FHA loans in the building: 10% of units in projects of 20 or more, and two loans in smaller ones</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-versus-card is-b">
    <p class="bl-versus-head">VA</p>
    <p class="bl-versus-big">Its own approved list</p>
    <ul class="bl-versus-list">
      <li>Look the project up in VA's condo report</li>
      <li>Being on the FHA list doesn't put it on the VA list</li>
      <li>If it isn't listed, ask your loan officer about requesting a review before you count on a VA loan</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

## What to ask for before you offer

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>This year's budget and the balance sheet</b> Divide the annual reserve contribution by the assessment income. That's the percentage in the table above.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>The latest reserve study, if there is one</b> It shows what big repairs are coming and whether the money exists to pay for them.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>A year of board meeting minutes</b> Roofs, leaks, lawsuits and upcoming assessments show up here before they show up anywhere else.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>The insurance certificate</b> Find out what the master policy covers and where it stops. You'll normally need your own HO-6 policy for what's inside your walls.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Ask directly about special assessments</b> Get the purpose, amount, remaining balance and payoff date. If one is tied to a critical repair that hasn't been fixed, the project is ineligible.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Don't assume approval lasts</b> Project reviews expire, and a condo that qualified for last year's buyer can fail for you. It's checked again on every loan.</span></div>
</div>
