Many first homes are bought with a little help from family. That's completely allowed. What lenders need is proof that the money really is a gift, that it comes from an acceptable person, and that you don't have to pay it back. Without that, a generous gift can turn into a closing delay.

## Who can give you the money

<div class="bl-versus">
  <div class="bl-versus-card is-a">
    <p class="bl-versus-head">FHA</p>
    <p class="bl-versus-big">A broad list</p>
    <ul class="bl-versus-list">
      <li>A family member</li>
      <li>Your employer or labor union</li>
      <li>A close friend with a clearly defined and documented interest in you</li>
      <li>A charitable organization</li>
      <li>A government agency or public entity with a homeownership program for low- or moderate-income families or first-time buyers</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-versus-card is-b">
    <p class="bl-versus-head">Conventional (Fannie Mae)</p>
    <p class="bl-versus-big">Relatives, and close to it</p>
    <ul class="bl-versus-list">
      <li>Relatives</li>
      <li>Some non-relatives with a family-type relationship, such as a domestic partner or fiancé</li>
      <li>Never someone tied to the sale: the builder, the developer, the real estate agent or another interested party</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

On FHA, the person giving the gift can't use **cash on hand**, meaning money kept at home outside a bank. The funds have to come from a documented account.

## How much of the purchase a gift can cover

On a conventional loan, it depends on the property:

<div class="bl-table-wrap">
  <table class="bl-table">
    <thead><tr><th>Property</th><th>Can the gift cover everything?</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>One-unit principal residence</td><td class="bl-yes">Yes, at any loan-to-value</td></tr>
      <tr><td>2–4 unit principal residence or second home, with 20% or more down</td><td class="bl-yes">Yes</td></tr>
      <tr><td>2–4 unit principal residence or second home, with less than 20% down</td><td>You must put in at least 5% of your own money first</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Investment property</td><td class="bl-no">Gifts aren't allowed</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

A gift can go toward the down payment, closing costs or reserves, within those rules.

## The gift letter: what it must say

The lender will ask for a letter signed by the person giving the gift. It must include:

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>The amount</b> The actual amount, or the maximum they plan to give.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>That no repayment is expected</b> This is the heart of it. If you have to pay it back, it's a loan, and it counts against you.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Who they are</b> Their name, address, phone number and relationship to you.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>On FHA, both signatures</b> The letter is signed and dated by the donor and by you.</span></div>
</div>

Your lender will usually have a form for this, so ask for it instead of writing your own.

## The paper trail: how to move the money

The lender has to see the money leave the donor's account and arrive where it's going. The cleanest options are:

<div class="bl-steps">
  <div class="bl-step is-accent">
    <div class="bl-step-num">1</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">Before closing</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">Transfer to your account</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">A check or electronic transfer from the donor's account to yours. Keep the donor's statement showing the withdrawal and yours showing the deposit.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">2</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">At closing</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">Directly to the title company</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">The donor sends a wire or cashier's check to the closing agent. It still has to be documented, and the money must arrive in time.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

Whichever route you choose, confirm the wiring instructions by phone before anyone sends money. Real estate wire fraud is common, and a gift is a big target. See <a href="/blog/closing-wire-fraud">wire fraud at closing</a>.

## Taxes: who pays, if anyone

For 2026, the IRS annual gift exclusion is **$19,000 per person receiving a gift**. A married couple giving together can give $38,000 to one person without going over.

Going over doesn't automatically mean anyone owes tax. The **donor**, not you, is generally responsible for any gift tax, and may need to file a gift tax return (Form 709). Your parents should ask a tax professional before giving a large amount.

## A gift of equity: when family sells to family

If a parent or another relative is selling you the home, they can give you part of their equity instead of cash. For example, they sell you a $400,000 home for $360,000, and the $40,000 difference counts toward your down payment. On FHA, only family members can give equity this way, and on Fannie Mae a gift of equity can't be used for reserves.
