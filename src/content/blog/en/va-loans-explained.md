If you served, a VA loan is usually the strongest mortgage you can get: no down payment required in most cases, no monthly mortgage insurance, and competitive rates. It isn't free, though, and the details decide how much it really saves you.

## What makes a VA loan different

<div class="bl-stats">
  <div class="bl-stat"><div class="bl-stat-num">$0</div><div class="bl-stat-label">Down payment, in most cases, if your entitlement covers the loan</div></div>
  <div class="bl-stat"><div class="bl-stat-num">No PMI</div><div class="bl-stat-label">There is no monthly mortgage insurance on a VA loan</div></div>
  <div class="bl-stat is-accent"><div class="bl-stat-num">25%</div><div class="bl-stat-label">The share of the loan VA guarantees to the lender on loans above $144,000</div></div>
</div>

That guarantee is the reason lenders can lend with no down payment. The cost of it is the **funding fee**, charged once, at closing.

## The funding fee

It's a percentage of the loan amount, and it depends on your down payment and on whether you've used a VA loan before.

### Who doesn't pay it

You're exempt from the funding fee if you:

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Receive VA compensation for a service-connected disability</b> Or you're eligible for it but receive retirement or active-duty pay instead.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Are a surviving spouse receiving Dependency and Indemnity Compensation</b></span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Are a service member with a proposed or memorandum rating before closing</b> Based on a pre-discharge claim.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Are on active duty and provide evidence of a Purple Heart</b> On or before closing.</span></div>
</div>

If you're later awarded compensation retroactive to before your closing date, you may be able to get the fee refunded. A rating received after closing doesn't count.

## Entitlement: how much you can borrow with no down payment

Your **entitlement** is how much VA will guarantee for you. With full entitlement, there's no VA loan limit: the lender decides how much you qualify for based on your income, credit and debts, and the loan can't be more than the price or the appraised value, whichever is lower.

If you've used part of your entitlement and haven't restored it, the math changes. VA's example:

<div class="bl-steps">
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">1</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-title">Start with the county limit × 25%</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">With a $900,000 county limit, that's $225,000.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">2</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-title">Subtract the entitlement you're already using</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">$225,000 − $50,000 = $175,000 remaining.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step is-accent">
    <div class="bl-step-num">3</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-title">Multiply by 4</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">$175,000 × 4 = $700,000. That's the most most lenders would lend with no down payment. Above that, they'd likely ask for a down payment.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

## Residual income: VA's extra test

Besides your debt-to-income ratio, VA looks at **residual income**: the money left over each month after your housing payment, debts and estimated living costs. The required amount depends on your family size and region. It means a large family can need more income to qualify than the same DTI would suggest on another loan.

<div class="bl-callout">
  <p class="bl-callout-title">Pedro's note</p>
  <p>Ask your seller for concessions. VA doesn't limit what a seller can pay toward your normal closing costs, and seller concessions beyond that, like paying off a debt or your funding fee, are allowed up to 4% of the home's reasonable value. With a disability rating that exempts you from the fee, the cash you need at closing can be very small. Get your Certificate of Eligibility early, so we know exactly where you stand before you make an offer.</p>
