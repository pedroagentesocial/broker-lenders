Of everything that can go wrong in a home purchase, this is the only one that can take all of your money in a single afternoon, with no way to undo it. It doesn't happen through a bad loan or a bad house. It starts with an email that looks completely normal.

## How big the problem is

<div class="bl-stats">
  <div class="bl-stat"><div class="bl-stat-num">12,368</div><div class="bl-stat-label">Real estate fraud complaints to the FBI in 2025</div></div>
  <div class="bl-stat"><div class="bl-stat-num">$275 million</div><div class="bl-stat-label">In reported losses that year</div></div>
  <div class="bl-stat is-accent"><div class="bl-stat-num">+58%</div><div class="bl-stat-label">More in losses than in 2024, which saw about $174 million</div></div>
</div>

Those are only the cases people reported. In one 2025 case described in the FBI report, a couple about to close on their home received an email impersonating their own attorneys and wired more than $449,000.

## How the scam works

<div class="bl-steps">
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">1</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-title">Someone watches the conversation</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">Scammers break into or imitate an email account belonging to an agent, a title company, an attorney or the buyer. They read the messages and wait for the right moment.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">2</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-title">A "last-minute change" arrives</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">Days before closing, you get an email with the real logo, real names and real details of your purchase. It says there's a new account for the wire.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">3</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-title">It pressures you to move fast</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">"Send it today or you'll lose the closing date." Urgency stops you from checking.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step is-accent">
    <div class="bl-step-num">4</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-title">The money goes to the scammer</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">A wire moves quickly and is hard to reverse. By the time the real title company asks where your funds are, the money may already have been moved.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

## Warning signs

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Any change to wiring instructions</b> Title companies very rarely change accounts in the middle of a transaction. Treat any change as fraud until you confirm it by phone.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Urgency or secrecy</b> "Don't call, I'm in meetings." "It has to go out in the next hour."</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>An address that's almost right</b> One letter changed, or a different domain ending. Scammers count on you not noticing.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>A phone number inside the same email</b> If you call the number in a fraudulent email, you're calling the scammer.</span></div>
</div>

## Five habits that protect your money

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Choose two trusted contacts early</b> Usually your agent and your title or settlement agent. Get their phone numbers in person or from a source you already trust, at the start of the process.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Confirm instructions by phone before you wire</b> Call a number you already had, not one from the email, and read back the account name and number.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Consider a code phrase</b> Agree on a word with the title company. If an email or call doesn't know it, it's not them.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Don't send financial details by email</b> Account numbers and personal documents should go through a secure portal.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Call to confirm the money arrived</b> Right after you wire, call the title company at its known number to confirm receipt.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="bl-callout">
  <p class="bl-callout-title">Pedro's note</p>
  <p>Wiring instructions come from the title or settlement company, and they shouldn't change with an email the day before closing. If you get something that looks like that, even if it seems to come from me or anyone else in the deal, don't wire. Call me at the number you already have. ## If you already sent the money: minutes matter

<div class="bl-steps">
  <div class="bl-step is-accent">
    <div class="bl-step-num">1</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">Right away</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">Call your bank and ask for a wire recall</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">Use the number on your card or the bank's website. Say clearly that it's fraud and ask them to contact the receiving bank.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">2</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">The same day</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">File a complaint at ic3.gov</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center has a team that works with banks to try to freeze stolen funds. The faster you report, the better the chances.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">3</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-meta">Next</p>
      <p class="bl-step-title">Let everyone in the transaction know</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">Your agent, the title company and your lender. Someone's email may be compromised, and other people in the deal may be at risk too.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
