"Closing costs" sounds like one fee. It's about forty of them, paid to at least six different parties, and only a fraction of it actually goes to the lender. Understanding which part is which is the difference between negotiating effectively and just feeling robbed.

On a $400,000 purchase, plan on roughly 2% to 5% of the price. Here's what a middle-of-the-road $12,000 actually consists of.

## What can change between the estimate and the table

This is the part almost nobody knows, and it's the most useful thing in this article. Federal rules put every fee on your Loan Estimate into one of three buckets, and each bucket has different protection.

<div class="bl-table-wrap">
  <table class="bl-table">
    <thead><tr><th>Bucket</th><th>Can it increase?</th><th>What's in it</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>Zero tolerance</td><td class="bl-yes">No — not by a dollar</td><td>The lender's own fees (origination, underwriting), transfer taxes, and any service you weren't allowed to shop for</td></tr>
      <tr><td>10% tolerance</td><td>Only as a group, and only by 10%</td><td>Recording fees, and services you could shop for if you picked a provider from the lender's written list</td></tr>
      <tr><td>No tolerance</td><td class="bl-no">Yes</td><td>Prepaid interest, homeowners insurance premiums, escrow deposits, and any provider you chose off-list</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

If a zero-tolerance fee is higher on your Closing Disclosure than it was on your Loan Estimate, the lender has to cure the difference. That is not a favor — it's the rule. Which is exactly why you should keep your Loan Estimate and compare it line by line against the Closing Disclosure you get three business days before closing.

<div class="bl-callout">
  <p class="bl-callout-title">Pedro's note</p>
  <p>Get Loan Estimates from more than one lender on the same day, and compare page 2, not page 1. Page 1 is the rate everyone advertises. Page 2 is where the fees live, and it's where a "lower rate" quietly becomes more expensive. It's a standardized form for exactly this reason — use it that way.</p>
</div>

## How much the seller can pay for you

Seller-paid closing costs are the most under-used tool in the whole process. Every program caps them, and the cap is bigger than most buyers realize.

  <p class="bl-fig-note">These are ceilings, not entitlements — the seller still has to agree. On a VA loan the 4% cap applies to concessions specifically; a seller may also pay your reasonable and customary closing costs on top of that. And a seller can never fund your down payment.</p>

Here's the trade most buyers never think to make: **ask for a credit instead of a discount.** On a $400,000 house, $10,000 off the price saves you about $63 a month. $10,000 toward your closing costs saves you $10,000 today — cash you keep, in the account, the week you move in.

## Five ways to actually lower the number

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Shop the title company</b> Title and settlement is 25% of the bill and you are allowed to choose the provider. Quotes vary by hundreds of dollars for identical coverage.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Ask for a lender credit</b> A slightly higher rate can come with a credit that covers most of the lender fees. On a loan you'll refinance in five years, that's often the better math.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Close near the end of the month</b> Prepaid interest runs from closing day to month end. Closing on the 28th instead of the 3rd cuts that line to almost nothing.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Shop insurance before you're under contract</b> Homeowners premiums vary widely, the first year is prepaid at closing, and nobody has time to compare in the last week.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Don't skip the owner's title policy</b> It's the one line people cut. It's also the only thing that protects <em>you</em> — the lender's policy protects the lender.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Don't confuse the inspection with the appraisal</b> The appraisal is a lender requirement about value. The home inspection is optional, costs $400–$700, and is the best money you'll spend all year.</span></div>
</div>

## Before you sign

You get your Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. That waiting period exists so you can read it. Put it next to your Loan Estimate, check the three buckets above, and ask about anything that moved.
