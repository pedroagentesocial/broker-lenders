You'll hear that you should refinance when rates drop a full point. That rule is wrong often enough to be useless — it ignores what the refinance costs, how much you still owe, and how long you're staying.

There's only one calculation, and it takes thirty seconds:

> **Total cost of the refinance ÷ monthly savings = the number of months until it pays for itself.**

If you'll be in the loan longer than that number, refinance. If not, don't. Everything else is commentary.

## The four reasons that are actually good

<div class="bl-table-wrap">
  <table class="bl-table">
    <thead><tr><th>Reason</th><th>What to check</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>Lower the rate</td><td>The break-even above. Nothing else.</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Get rid of FHA mortgage insurance</td><td>With under 10% down, FHA MIP lasts the life of the loan. If you now have 20% equity and a 680+ score, moving to conventional can save $200+ a month <em>even if the rate is the same</em>. This is the most under-used refinance there is.</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Shorten the term</td><td>Going 30 to 15 raises the payment but can cut total interest dramatically. Only do it if the higher payment is comfortable — you can't undo it without another refinance.</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Take cash out</td><td>Usually capped around 80% of the home's value. Compare the rate against what you'd pay on the debt you're consolidating, and be honest about whether the credit cards stay at zero.</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

## The three traps

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Restarting the 30-year clock</b> If you're eight years into a 30-year loan and you refinance into a new 30, you just added eight years of payments. A lower rate on a longer term can cost more total interest. Ask for a 22-year term instead — most lenders will do custom terms and almost nobody offers it unprompted.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>"No-cost" refinances</b> There is no such thing. The costs are either rolled into your balance or paid for with a higher rate. That can be the right choice — just make sure you know which one you picked and run the break-even on the real numbers.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Refinancing to consolidate, then re-borrowing</b> Rolling $30,000 of credit card debt into your mortgage turns unsecured debt into debt secured by your house. It works — once — and only if the cards stay paid off.</span></div>
</div>

One tax note, since it comes up: points paid on a purchase are generally deductible in the year you pay them, but points paid on a refinance are generally deducted a little each year over the life of the loan. Talk to your tax preparer before assuming a deduction.

Use the [refinance calculator](/calculators/refinance-calculator) to get your monthly savings, ask for a Loan Estimate to get the real cost, and divide. If the answer is fewer months than you plan to keep the loan, it's worth doing — and if it isn't, I'll tell you that too.
