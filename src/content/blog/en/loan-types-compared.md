People shop for houses. Almost nobody shops for loan programs — and the program is where the money actually is. The same $400,000 house financed five different ways produces five different monthly payments and, over ten years, tens of thousands of dollars of difference.

The good news: choosing is mostly mechanical. Four questions, in order.

## The factor most people miss: how long you pay mortgage insurance

Everyone compares interest rates. Almost nobody compares how many years they'll be paying mortgage insurance — and that difference is usually larger.

<figure class="bl-fig">
  <p class="bl-fig-title">Years of mortgage insurance, by program</p>
  <p class="bl-fig-sub">On a 30-year fixed loan, assuming you make normal payments and don't refinance.</p>
  <div class="bl-bars">
    <div class="bl-bar"><div class="bl-bar-label">VA <small>Any down payment</small></div><div class="bl-bar-track"><div class="bl-bar-fill is-outside" style="--w:0%;--c:#dc2626" data-value="0 years"></div></div></div>
    <div class="bl-bar"><div class="bl-bar-label">Conventional <small>3% down</small></div><div class="bl-bar-track"><div class="bl-bar-fill" style="--w:37%;--c:#dc2626">~11 years</div></div></div>
    <div class="bl-bar"><div class="bl-bar-label">FHA <small>10%+ down</small></div><div class="bl-bar-track"><div class="bl-bar-fill" style="--w:37%;--c:#374151">11 years</div></div></div>
    <div class="bl-bar"><div class="bl-bar-label">FHA <small>Under 10% down</small></div><div class="bl-bar-track"><div class="bl-bar-fill" style="--w:100%;--c:#111827">Life of the loan</div></div></div>
    <div class="bl-bar"><div class="bl-bar-label">USDA <small>0% down</small></div><div class="bl-bar-track"><div class="bl-bar-fill" style="--w:100%;--c:#9ca3af">Life of the loan</div></div></div>
  </div>
  <p class="bl-fig-note">Conventional PMI comes off by law: you can request cancellation at 80% of original value and it terminates automatically at 78%, or at the midpoint of the amortization schedule at the latest. FHA's annual MIP has no such exit below 10% down — the only way out is a refinance.</p>
</figure>

That's the whole conventional-versus-FHA decision in one chart. On a $388,000 loan, $200 a month of mortgage insurance for eleven years is about $26,000. For thirty years it's over $70,000.

<div class="bl-callout">
  <p class="bl-callout-title">Pedro's note</p>
  <p>FHA is not the "bad" loan and conventional isn't the "good" one. FHA is a door — it opens at 580 credit and it forgives a lot. What I tell clients is: if FHA is the door you need today, take it, and put a note in your calendar. When your score and your equity catch up, we refinance out of the MIP. That's a plan, not a compromise.</p>
</div>

## The three cases that break the flowchart

<div class="bl-steps">
  <div class="bl-step is-accent">
    <div class="bl-step-num">1</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-title">The house needs work</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">A home that won't pass a standard appraisal — bad roof, no working systems — may not finance conventionally at all. FHA 203(k) and renovation loans exist for exactly this, and they let you finance the repairs into the loan.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">2</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-title">You're self-employed</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">Program choice matters less than how your income is documented. Two years of returns with heavy write-offs can qualify you for far less than your bank balance suggests. This is worth a conversation before you shop, not after.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">3</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-title">You have VA eligibility and didn't think it applied</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">Guard and Reserve service can qualify. Surviving spouses can qualify. Having used a VA loan before does not disqualify you — entitlement can be restored or partially reused. Ask before you assume.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
