Most of the friction in a mortgage isn't the decision — it's the paperwork chase. Files don't get denied for having the wrong documents. They get delayed, over and over, for not having them yet.

Here is the whole list, up front, so you can gather it once.

## What everyone provides

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Government photo ID</b> Driver's license or passport, matching the name you'll close in.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Two months of bank statements</b> All pages, including the blank last page. Every account you're using for the down payment or reserves.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Two years of address history</b> With landlord contact info if you've been renting.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Retirement and investment statements</b> Most recent quarter, if you're using them for reserves or the down payment.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Explanations for anything unusual</b> A late payment, a collection, a name change, a gap in employment. A short signed letter clears most of them.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Gift letter, if applicable</b> Signed by the donor, stating the funds are a gift with no repayment expected, plus proof of the transfer.</span></div>
</div>

## Then it splits

<div class="bl-versus">
  <div class="bl-versus-card is-a">
    <p class="bl-versus-head">If you're a W-2 employee</p>
    <p class="bl-versus-big">About 5 documents</p>
    <ul class="bl-versus-list">
      <li>30 days of pay stubs, most recent</li>
      <li>W-2s for the last two years</li>
      <li>Two years of tax returns if you have commission, bonus, or rental income</li>
      <li>Employer name, address and HR phone — employment is verified independently, twice</li>
      <li>If you changed jobs recently: an offer letter and your first pay stub in the new role</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-versus-card is-b">
    <p class="bl-versus-head">If you're self-employed</p>
    <p class="bl-versus-big">About 10 documents</p>
    <ul class="bl-versus-list">
      <li>Two years of <em>personal</em> federal tax returns, all schedules</li>
      <li>Two years of <em>business</em> returns if you file separately (1120, 1120S, 1065) plus K-1s</li>
      <li>Year-to-date profit and loss statement, and a balance sheet if requested</li>
      <li>Three months of business bank statements</li>
      <li>Business license or CPA letter confirming the business is active</li>
      <li>Signed authorization for the lender to pull your IRS transcripts</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

Self-employed is not harder to approve. It's just a longer conversation, and it needs to start earlier.

## How self-employed income is actually calculated

This is the number that surprises people, and it's the reason to talk to a lender in January rather than in June.

<figure class="bl-fig">
  <p class="bl-fig-title">What the underwriter sees vs. what the business earns</p>
  <p class="bl-fig-sub">A sole proprietor filing Schedule C. Illustrative, but this is the actual sequence.</p>
  <div class="bl-bars">
    <div class="bl-bar"><div class="bl-bar-label">Gross receipts <small>What the business took in</small></div><div class="bl-bar-track"><div class="bl-bar-fill" style="--w:100%;--c:#9ca3af">$180,000</div></div></div>
    <div class="bl-bar"><div class="bl-bar-label">Net profit <small>After write-offs, Schedule C line 31</small></div><div class="bl-bar-track"><div class="bl-bar-fill" style="--w:53%;--c:#dc2626">$95,000</div></div></div>
    <div class="bl-bar"><div class="bl-bar-label">Qualifying income <small>After allowable add-backs</small></div><div class="bl-bar-track"><div class="bl-bar-fill" style="--w:59%;--c:#111827">$105,500</div></div></div>
  </div>
  <p class="bl-fig-note">$105,500 a year — $8,792 a month — is what your debt-to-income ratio is calculated on. Not the $180,000. Every dollar of aggressive write-off is a dollar of buying power, and that trade is worth planning two years before you buy.</p>
</figure>

Some deductions come back, because they aren't real cash leaving your account:

<div class="bl-table-wrap">
  <table class="bl-table">
    <thead><tr><th>Line item</th><th>Treatment</th><th>Why</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>Depreciation</td><td class="bl-yes">Added back</td><td>A paper expense — no money actually left the business</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Depletion and amortization</td><td class="bl-yes">Added back</td><td>Same reason</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Business use of home</td><td class="bl-yes">Usually added back</td><td>The expense exists whether or not you run a business there</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Non-deductible meals portion</td><td class="bl-no">Subtracted</td><td>Real money spent that the return doesn't fully show</td></tr>
      <tr><td>One-time capital gain or loss</td><td class="bl-no">Removed</td><td>Not recurring income, so it can't be counted</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Vehicle mileage depreciation</td><td class="bl-yes">Partly added back</td><td>A portion of the standard mileage rate is depreciation</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

Income is generally averaged over 24 months. If year two is lower than year one, most guidelines require the lower or the most recent figure — a declining trend can't be averaged away.

## The two rules that cause the most delays

<div class="bl-steps">
  <div class="bl-step is-accent">
    <div class="bl-step-num">1</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-title">Every large deposit needs a source</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">Anything that isn't payroll and stands out against your normal pattern will be questioned. You'll need to show where it came from — a cancelled check, a bill of sale, a transfer record. Cash you deposited from home has no trail and generally can't be used at all. Money that's been in your account more than 60 days is usually seasoned and stops being an issue.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="bl-step">
    <div class="bl-step-num">2</div>
    <div class="bl-step-body">
      <p class="bl-step-title">Documents expire</p>
      <p class="bl-step-text">Pay stubs, bank statements and credit reports all age out — commonly at 60 to 120 days depending on the item. If your closing gets pushed, expect to send updated copies. It isn't the lender being difficult; the file has to be current on the day it funds.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

## Do this before you apply

Make one folder. Scan everything as PDFs — all pages, no phone photos of a screen. Name the files plainly. It takes an evening, and it turns a three-week pre-approval into a two-day one.
