A bankruptcy or a foreclosure feels permanent. For a mortgage, it isn't. Every major loan program has a waiting period, and when it ends, you can be approved like anyone else who meets the requirements.

The surprise is how much that wait depends on the loan. After a foreclosure, the standard conventional wait is seven years. On an FHA loan it's three. Choosing the right program can put you in a home years sooner.

Two details matter here. If a mortgage was included in a bankruptcy and later foreclosed, Fannie Mae may apply the bankruptcy period instead of the foreclosure period, but only if your documents show that debt was discharged. Otherwise the longer period applies. Keep your discharge papers.

The end of the clock also differs. On a manually underwritten Fannie Mae loan, the period runs until the new loan is disbursed. On FHA, it's measured when your case number is assigned, which happens early in the process.

## What counts as extenuating circumstances

It doesn't mean "a hard time." Fannie Mae defines it as a nonrecurring event beyond your control that caused a sudden, significant and prolonged drop in income, or a catastrophic increase in financial obligations. FHA's examples include a serious illness or the death of a wage earner.

You have to prove it, with three kinds of paperwork:

Divorce is a good example of why the program matters. Fannie Mae lists a divorce decree among the documents that can support a claim. FHA says divorce alone isn't an extenuating circumstance, although it can allow an exception if your mortgage was current at the time of the divorce, your ex-spouse received the home, and the loan was foreclosed afterward.

## The wait is only half of it

The end of the waiting period doesn't approve you by itself. You still have to meet the credit, income and down payment rules. Fannie Mae also requires traditional credit accounts, so a thin file won't work. FHA looks for good credit reestablished since the bankruptcy, or a choice not to take on new credit.
