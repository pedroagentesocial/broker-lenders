A credit score is not a report card. Nobody at the lender is judging your character. The score does exactly one thing: it sets a price. And because it sets a price, the difference between a 679 and a 680 can be worth more than a raise.

Here's what that looks like in real numbers.

## The thresholds that actually change something

Scores don't work on a smooth slope. They work in bands, and the jumps between bands are where the money is.

## The 60-day plan that actually works

<div class="bl-checks">
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Pay balances below 30% of the limit — then below 10%</b> Utilization is recalculated every statement cycle. This is the single fastest legitimate way to move a score.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Pay before the statement closes, not before the due date</b> The balance reported to the bureaus is the statement balance. Paying on the 25th when your statement cuts on the 20th reports the high number.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Pull all three reports free at AnnualCreditReport.com</b> Errors are common. Disputing a wrong late payment or a debt that isn't yours can move a score in weeks.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check"><span class="bl-check-icon">✓</span><span><b>Ask to be added as an authorized user</b> On a family member's old card with a perfect history and low utilization. It borrows their age and their behavior.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Don't close old cards</b> Closing a card shrinks your total available credit — which raises your utilization — and shortens your average account age. Both hurt.</span></div>
  <div class="bl-check is-warn"><span class="bl-check-icon">!</span><span><b>Don't open anything new</b> No car, no furniture financing, no store card, from the day you start shopping until the day you get keys. Not even zero percent.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="bl-callout">
  <p class="bl-callout-title">Pedro's note</p>
  <p>The most expensive mistake I see is a client who gets pre-approved, then buys a car before closing. The lender re-pulls credit days before funding. A new $600 car payment on a file approved at 44% DTI can kill the loan the week of closing — and by then the moving truck is booked. Between pre-approval and keys, buy nothing on credit. Nothing.</p>
</div>

## One more thing about shopping around

People avoid getting quotes from more than one lender because they're afraid of the credit hit. Don't be. Mortgage inquiries made in a short shopping window are treated as a single inquiry by the scoring models — the whole point is to let you compare. One extra quote costs you nothing and can be worth $46,000.
